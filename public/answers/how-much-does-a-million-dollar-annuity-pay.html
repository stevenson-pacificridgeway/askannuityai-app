How much does a $1 million annuity pay per month?

Short answer: It depends on the product, today's rates, your age, and when income starts — but a fixed indexed annuity with a lifetime income rider commonly illustrates around 5%–6% of the premium per year. On $1,000,000 that's roughly $50,000–$60,000 a year (about $4,200–$5,000 a month) guaranteed for life. These are illustrative figures, not a quote — your exact number depends on the carrier, the rider, your starting age, and whether it covers one life or two.

What changes the number

  • Your age when income starts. Older starting ages and longer waits generally mean a larger monthly payout.
  • Single vs. joint. Covering two lives (you and a spouse) pays a bit less because it's guaranteed over two lifetimes.
  • The product and rider. An immediate annuity, a fixed annuity, and a fixed indexed annuity with an income rider all pay differently.
  • Current rates. Insurer payout rates move over time, so any quote is only good for a window.

Why "about 5–6%" is a useful rule of thumb

For lifetime-income annuities, a common illustration lands somewhere in the 5%–6%-per-year range depending on age and timing. That's why $500,000 often illustrates near $2,100–$2,500 a month and $1,000,000 roughly doubles it.
