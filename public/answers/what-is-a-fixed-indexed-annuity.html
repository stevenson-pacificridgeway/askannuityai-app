What is a fixed indexed annuity (and how does it work)?

Short answer: A fixed indexed annuity (FIA) is a long-term insurance contract with two key features: principal protection — your money doesn't go backward when the market drops — and growth potential linked to part of a market index without actually being invested in the market. In exchange for the downside protection, gains are limited by a cap, participation rate, or spread. Add a lifetime income rider and it can also pay you a guaranteed paycheck for life.

The two features that make it popular

  • Principal protection. In a year the index falls, your credited interest is simply zero — you don't lose money to market losses.
  • Index-linked growth. In a year the index rises, you earn a portion of that gain. You're never directly invested in stocks, so you trade some upside for safety.

How the growth is calculated

Insurers limit the upside using one of a few methods: a cap (a maximum credited rate), a participation rate (you get a percentage of the index gain), or a spread (a set amount subtracted from the gain). That trade-off is the price of never going backward — it's built for safety and predictability, not for maximizing market returns.

Adding guaranteed lifetime income

Many people add a guaranteed lifetime income rider for a small annual fee. It turns the FIA into a paycheck you can't outlive, and the longer you wait before turning income on, the larger that future paycheck usually is. That's what makes FIAs a common tool for turning a 401(k) into guaranteed income.

Who it fits — and who it doesn't

An FIA can fit someone at or near retirement who wants protected growth and a reliable income floor for essential bills. It's usually used for part of your savings, not all of it, because the money is less liquid and has a surrender period. If you need full access to your money short-term, or you're decades from retirement, other tools may come first.
