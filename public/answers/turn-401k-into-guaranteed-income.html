How do I turn my 401(k) into guaranteed income?

Short answer: The usual path is a direct rollover of some or all of your 401(k) into an IRA annuity with a lifetime income rider. Done trustee-to-trustee, it's generally not a taxable event, and the annuity then pays you a guaranteed amount every month for life. Many people convert only part of their 401(k) — enough to cover essential bills — and keep the rest invested for growth.

The step-by-step path

  1. Decide how much to convert. A common approach is to cover your must-pay bills with guaranteed income and leave the rest invested. You rarely need to convert all of it.
  2. Choose the vehicle. For lifetime income, that's typically a fixed indexed annuity with an income rider, or an immediate annuity if you want income to start right away.
  3. Do a direct rollover. Move the money trustee-to-trustee from your 401(k) into an IRA annuity. Because the money never touches your hands, it's generally not taxed at rollover.
  4. Turn on income when you're ready. You can start the paycheck now, or let the income base grow and start later for a larger amount.

What to watch for on taxes

A direct rollover avoids the 20% withholding and potential penalties that can hit an indirect (60-day) rollover. Because a traditional 401(k) is pre-tax, the income you later receive is generally taxable as ordinary income — just like 401(k) withdrawals would have been. Everyone's tax picture is different, so confirm the details with a licensed professional or tax advisor before you move anything.

The trade-offs, honestly

  • Upside: a paycheck you can't outlive, principal protection from market drops, and predictable budgeting.
  • Trade-off: money moved into an income annuity is less liquid and has a surrender period, which is why most people convert only part of their savings.
