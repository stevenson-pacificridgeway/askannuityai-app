Will I run out of money in retirement?

Short answer: You can — but mostly if you rely only on drawing down savings. Two risks drive it: living longer than your money lasts, and a market drop at the wrong time. The most reliable fix is to cover your essential bills with guaranteed lifetime income (Social Security plus, for many, an annuity with a lifetime income rider), so a core paycheck keeps coming no matter how long you live or what the market does.

The two risks that cause it

  • Longevity risk. People are living longer — your savings may need to last 30 years or more. Plan for a long life, not an average one.
  • Sequence-of-returns risk. A big loss early in retirement, while you're taking withdrawals, can permanently shrink your nest egg. The same average return can leave you fine or broke depending on when the bad years hit.

Why the "4% rule" isn't a guarantee

The old rule of thumb — withdraw about 4% a year — is a planning estimate, not a promise. It can fall short if you live a long time, if early returns are poor, or if inflation runs hot. It's a starting point, not a safety net.

How a guaranteed income floor prevents running out

The dependable way to make running out nearly impossible is to build an income "floor" that covers your must-pay bills — housing, food, insurance, utilities — with income that's guaranteed for life. For many retirees that's Social Security plus an annuity with a lifetime income rider. Your essentials are covered no matter what, and the rest of your savings can stay invested for growth and flexibility.
