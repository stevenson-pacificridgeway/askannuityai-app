Are annuities a good idea? Honest pros and cons

Short answer: For the right person and the right portion of savings, yes. The pros: guaranteed lifetime income you can't outlive, protection from market losses, and predictable budgeting. The cons: fees, reduced liquidity, and surrender periods. They fit people at or near retirement who want a reliable income floor for essential bills — and they're usually used for only part of a portfolio, not all of it.

The pros

  • Income you can't outlive. With a lifetime income rider, the paycheck keeps coming no matter how long you live.
  • Principal protection. A fixed or fixed indexed annuity doesn't go backward when markets fall.
  • Predictability. A guaranteed floor makes budgeting in retirement far simpler and less stressful.
  • Protects against bad timing. It shields you from a market drop early in retirement while you're withdrawing.

The cons (said honestly)

  • Fees. Riders and certain annuity types carry costs — you should know exactly what you're paying and what it buys.
  • Liquidity. Money in an income annuity is less accessible, which is why you convert only part of your savings.
  • Surrender periods. Taking out more than the allowed amount early can trigger a surrender charge for a set number of years.
  • Not for growth-chasing. You trade some upside for safety, so it isn't the tool for maximizing market returns.

Who they fit — and who they don't

An annuity often fits someone near or in retirement who worries about running out of money and wants a guaranteed paycheck for the basics. It usually doesn't fit someone who needs all their money liquid short-term, or who is decades from retirement with time to ride out the market. The honest answer is "it depends" — on your age, your goals, and how much guaranteed income you actually need. A good next step is a stress-test of whether your plan can run out.
