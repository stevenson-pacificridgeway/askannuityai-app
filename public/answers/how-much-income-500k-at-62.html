How much monthly income could $500k give me at 62?

Short answer: It depends on the product, today's rates, and when you turn income on — but a fixed indexed annuity with a lifetime income rider starting at 62 commonly illustrates around 5%–6% of your premium per year. On $500,000 that's roughly $25,000–$30,000 a year (about $2,100–$2,500 a month) for life. These are illustrative figures, not a quote — your exact number depends on the carrier, the rider, and whether it covers one life or two.

What actually determines the number

There's no single "right" answer because several levers move it up or down:

  • When income starts. Starting at 62 vs. waiting to 65 or 70 changes the payout meaningfully.
  • The product and rider. A fixed indexed annuity with a guaranteed lifetime income rider works differently from an immediate annuity (SPIA) or a plain fixed annuity.
  • One life or two. A joint payout that covers you and a spouse pays a bit less, because it's guaranteed over two lifetimes.
  • Current rates. Insurance company payout rates change over time, so quotes are only good for a window.

A realistic illustrative range

Using the common 5%–6% lifetime payout range for income starting at 62, $500,000 often illustrates in the ballpark of $2,100 to $2,500 per month guaranteed for life. Think of that as a floor you can't outlive, which you'd layer on top of Social Security. The only way to know your real figure is a current illustration for your age, state, and the specific product — that's a free, no-obligation request.

Why waiting can grow your paycheck

With an income rider, the longer you wait before turning income on, the larger your future paycheck usually is, because the income base keeps growing in the meantime. Even waiting a few years can increase your lifetime monthly income — so "should I start at 62 or wait?" is one of the most valuable questions to run the numbers on.
